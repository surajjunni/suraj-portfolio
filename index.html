About

I'm a Senior Machine Learning Engineer at Twilio, where I own the evaluation and release infrastructure for 50+ production models. I work across the whole lifecycle of an LLM system, from fine-tuning foundation models to shipping the reliable, scalable pipelines that keep them trustworthy once they're live.

My work sits at the intersection of LLM engineering, evaluation science, and MLOps. I've built distributed benchmarking infrastructure that cut release cycles by 40%, RAG pipelines that reduced customer issue resolution time by 25%, and multi-modal anomaly detection systems reaching 82% alert precision at 100K-user scale.

Before Twilio, I earned a Master's in Computer Science from Arizona State University (GPA 3.75) and published research at EMNLP and SBP-BRiMS. My academic work was on robustness in visual question answering, where I built evaluation benchmarks for domain generalization. That problem still shapes how I think about model reliability today.

To me, evaluation is what makes an AI system trustworthy, not just a box you tick before a release. The best ML system isn't the one with the highest benchmark score. It's the one you can deploy, watch, and keep improving without losing sleep.

Research

Preprint · Sole Author · 2026

Workflow-Aware Caching for LLM Agent Pipelines

Position Aware Caching (PAC) organizes the cache around workflow positions instead of individual content keys, so repeated agent pipelines reuse their intermediate results far more often. On five real workflows built with LangGraph, CrewAI, and the OpenAI Agents SDK, it raised cache hit rate by 7 to 29 points and cut workflow latency by up to 21%.

Under Review
